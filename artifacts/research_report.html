Status note (October 2026). This is the early research and strategy report. Some parts describe the original concept, not the software as built. Where they differ, this box is correct:
  • Built and running: AEGIS coordinator + 4 specialist agents (PULSE heart rate/SpO₂, GENE IL-6, MIRROR sleep, SOLARIS radiation), rule-based detection, trend forecasts, cascade detection, PREVENT/PROTECT protocols, DeepSeek explanations with an automatic fact check, phone alert to the on-call flight surgeon.
  • Data: vitals are simulated (no wearable hardware). IL-6 is modelled from stress and radiation, not measured. Radiation is judged against the Mars-transit background measured by Curiosity RAD (1.84 mSv/day). Real NASA data (Inspiration4 / OSDR OSD-575, NTRS abstracts) is used to check the rules and as the RAG knowledge base; nothing is “trained” on it.
  • Prediction scope: in the demo AEGIS forecasts a cascade about 2–3 minutes before the third domain crosses its limit (the demo runs a compressed 6-minute build-up). On a mission the same trend method would work on hours-to-days data; that has not been validated.
  • Not built (concept only): HRV, CRP, cognitive tests (PVT/n-back), live DSCOVR/GOES feeds, RadLab dose model, z-score/EWMA baselines, genomic (TP53) analysis, a 30-second auto-execute override. PROTECT protocols are raised for a human to confirm.
  • Category strategy now: Best Mission Concept (primary), Best Use of Technology (secondary).

02 Challenge Analysis

Long-duration missions to Mars (18–24 month round trip) expose astronauts to compounding physiological and psychological stresses that ground teams cannot monitor in real time (12–24 minute signal lag). The challenge targets the gap between ISS-era medicine (daily ground support) and deep-space autonomy (no resupply, no emergency evacuation).

04 Winning Patterns

1
Deep integration with NASA's primary datasets — not just branding
Winners ingest raw NASA data (GeneLab omics, OSDR studies, DSCOVR telemetry) and process it themselves. Projects that reference NASA without direct API/data integration score poorly. Our path: Inspiration4 dataset directly.
2
Interactive, polished web-based demo with a live URL
Every global winner delivers a live web app or interactive demo — not a PDF or slideshow. Judges experience the project. Static reports do not win. SpaceGenes+ deployed to Framer and had a working URL on submission day.
3
ML/AI applied to time-series prediction or anomaly detection
Storm Prophet (LSTM), 42 QuakeHeroes (deep neural nets), SpaceGenes+ (synergy threshold algorithm) — the pattern is consistent across years. An ML component showing predictive capability (not just lookup) significantly boosts scores.
4
Multi-stressor interaction — not a single variable
SpaceGenes+ won for addressing radiation AND microgravity in combination, detecting non-additive interactions. Prior submissions studied one stressor in isolation. Multi-variable analysis is treated as scientifically more valuable.
5
Working demo > polish > novelty
A functioning MVP with a live URL beats a concept document with beautiful slides. Framer-hosted SpaceGenes+ is a working interactive tool, not a prototype description.
6
Data accessibility — make science usable by non-experts
LunarTech Ensemble made seismology accessible. SpaceGenes+ made genomics accessible to mission planners. For a health monitor challenge, the interface for the astronaut (not the scientist) matters.
7
Open Science alignment (NASA TOPS initiative)
Since 2023 the challenge has aligned with NASA's Transform to Open Science (TOPS) initiative. Open-source code on GitHub, reproducible analyses, and documented APIs are explicitly favoured by judges.
8
Clear Artemis/Mars mission relevance
Every winning project connects to a real NASA mission need. A health monitor framed as "what Artemis IV through Mars transit crews need" resonates with judges who work on those programs.

07 Gaps & Opportunities for Project Horizons

Gap 1 — Multi-stressor systemic integration
SpaceGenes+ addressed multi-stressor interaction at the molecular (genomics) level. No winning project has addressed it at the systemic/clinical level — combining radiation dose + bone density trend + cardiovascular deconditioning + behavioral indicators into a single integrated risk score.
→ Opportunity: A "crew health credit score" that synthesizes multi-modal biomarker streams into one actionable status.
Gap 2 — Behavioral health monitoring
Psychological health (mood, cognitive performance, sleep quality, crew cohesion) is NASA HRP's #1 long-duration risk, yet almost no Space Apps project has tackled it. Quantifiable indicators exist: reaction time tests, sentiment analysis, sleep stage data, cortisol levels.
→ Opportunity: Tasteful, consent-driven behavioral health module — potentially also wins "Most Inspirational."
Gap 3 — Crew-autonomous decision support (no ground team)
Most prior health monitoring projects implicitly assume ground team access. None have explicitly modeled the loss-of-signal scenario: what does the crew do when there is no one to call?
→ Opportunity: "Flight surgeon in a box" — AI-assisted system that runs entirely on-board, interprets trends, and gives explainable crew-side recommendations.
Gap 4 — Predictive early warning, not just current state display
Most biosensor dashboards show current vital signs. Predicting health events 48–72 hours in advance (impending cardiovascular event, radiation-induced immune suppression, behavioral health crisis onset) is the more valuable — and harder — problem.
→ Opportunity: LSTM-based predictive model on OSDR biomarker time-series — validated on Inspiration4 data.
Gap 5 — Radiation → immune → behavioral health triple linkage
NASA research confirms radiation suppresses immune function AND increases psychological stress markers (via cortisol and inflammatory cytokines). No hackathon team has built a model showing this causal chain.
→ Opportunity: Show environmental radiation dose → immune marker change → behavioral health risk score. Scientifically novel, directly actionable.
Gap 6 — Astronaut-facing UX (not researcher-facing)
SpaceGenes+ and similar winning projects build interfaces for researchers. The Astronaut Health Monitor challenge asks for tools the crew can use autonomously. A crew-facing interface has different UX requirements: clear alert hierarchy, no information overload, guided diagnostic flows.
→ Opportunity: Two-view design — researcher/physician mode (dense data) + crew mode (traffic-light status, plain-language alerts, guided check-ins).
Gap 7 — Longitudinal trend detection over mission timeline
Most projects focus on real-time monitoring. The medically meaningful signal for long-duration missions is the trend — is bone density declining faster than expected this month? Is collective crew mood trending down over 3 weeks?
→ Opportunity: Longitudinal trend analysis with anomaly detection against mission-specific (not population) baselines.
