AEGIS Accuracy Report

Second run, 11 Oct 2026, after the Step 1 fixes. Same tests as the first run, against the live code. AI model: DeepSeek (deepseek-flash). Scripts: eval/run_eval.py, aegis_verify.py, eval/verify_step1*.py. Raw output: eval/results.json (first run kept as eval/results.before-step1.json).

We test two things. First, whether the agents' rules give the right answer on real measured data from NASA. Second, whether the AI wording stays true to the data and sources it is given. The first run found two rules that were wrong. This run shows what changed after fixing them, including what still fails.

1 · How the fact checker works

Every DeepSeek recommendation passes through aegis_verify.py before it reaches the dashboard. It does not ask another AI whether the text looks right. Each check compares the text mechanically against exactly what the model was given.

NumbersEvery number must appear in the crew readings, thresholds or evidence (rounding allowed; "about" allows 5%). Advice durations like "2 hours" are exempt.
CitationsEach sentence that cites [n] must be about the same thing as passage n (MiniLM similarity ≥ 0.30).
ActionsEach action must name a valid approved step. The card then shows that step's approved text, not the AI's wording.
MedicinesNo medicine, supplement or dose unless it already appears in the approved steps or evidence.
Units (voice)Spoken numbers may only use mSv, pg/mL, bpm, %, mmHg or time units. Doses, Gy/Sv/rem and lab units are rejected.

If a draft fails, the model gets one retry with the problems listed. If it fails again, the card shows the rule-based advice and a Rules only badge instead of ✓ Fact-checked.

4 · Does the checker catch hallucinations?

We took the verified outputs and planted known errors in them: a changed reading, an off-topic claim pinned to a real source, an action from another protocol, or a medicine. The action test here uses the strict similarity check, to measure how well paraphrases can be policed.

Planted errorCaughtMissed
Wrong or invented number21 / 21None
Off-topic claim with a real citation45 / 45None
Medicine or dose45 / 45None
Action not in the approved steps (paraphrase check)73 / 84"Hard cardio workout", "oxygen mask" and "extra hour of sleep" passed as rewrites of nearby steps
Clean outputs left untouched8 / 9 passOne faithful rewording scored below the 0.40 similarity line

Similarity measures topic, not meaning, so the paraphrase check cannot tell "reduce exertion" from "do a hard workout". That is why the dashboard no longer shows the AI's wording of a step: the card displays the approved text, and the AI's role is limited to choosing and ordering steps and explaining why. The paraphrase check is kept only as an audit signal.

6 · What this does not prove

  • Simulated and modelled inputs. Vital signs come from a simulator and IL-6 is modelled. The tests show the rules behave correctly on real reference values, not that AEGIS works on real astronauts.
  • Small samples. 4 Inspiration4 crew, 8 radiation cases, 10 recommendation scenarios and 12 questions. Each result shows a direction, not a measured accuracy rate. The GENE thresholds were chosen on the same 4 people they are tested on.
  • Inspiration4 lasted 3 days in low Earth orbit, with blood drawn on the ground. Long missions may behave differently.
  • PREDICT is validated only on simulated streams. The 2.6-minute lead time comes from a compressed 6-minute demo. On a mission the same method would run on hours-to-days data; that has not been tested.
  • The citation check proves relevance, not truth. A sentence can be on topic and still misstate its source.
  • The demo recovery is scripted. After PREVENT-1, the simulator reverses the drift. The dashboard labels this.

7 · Still open

  1. Citations: prompt DeepSeek to leave calculated projections uncited, or allow the findings themselves as a citable source.
  2. Voice focus: map role words ("engineer", "medical officer") to crew members the way surnames are mapped.
  3. Action audit: a small entailment model would make the paraphrase check meaningful (one-time ~100 MB download). Not needed for safety now that steps are shown verbatim.
  4. Real data for PREDICT: replay a continuous physiological dataset (for example an analog-mission or bed-rest study) through the trend engine.
