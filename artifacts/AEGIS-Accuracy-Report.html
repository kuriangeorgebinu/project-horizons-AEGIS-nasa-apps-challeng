AEGIS Accuracy Report

Run on 11 Oct 2026 against the live code. AI model: DeepSeek (deepseek-flash). Scripts: eval/run_eval.py, aegis_verify.py. Raw output: eval/results.json.

We tested two different things. First, whether the agents' rules give the right answer on real measured data from NASA. Second, whether the AI wording stays true to the data and sources it is given. Here is what we found, including the parts that failed.

Findings at a glance

0 of 20AI recommendation drafts that invented a number. 1 of 12 voice answers did, and the checker caught and corrected it.
9 of 10AI recommendations that pass every fact check after one retry. The tenth falls back to the rule-based advice.
185 of 192Planted hallucinations the checker caught. Numbers, citations and medicines: all caught. Actions: 75 of 82.
5 of 5PULSE heart-rate and oxygen decisions that match the clinical definitions.
0 of 4Real post-flight IL-6 rises (Inspiration4) that GENE's fixed 8 pg/mL threshold detects.
2 of 5Real radiation environments where SOLARIS gives the right call. It orders shelter for normal deep-space cruise.

The AI layer holds up well: it rarely invents facts, and the checker blocks what it does invent. The weak spot is upstream. Two of the rules the AI explains (GENE's IL-6 threshold and SOLARIS's daily-dose threshold) do not match real data. A perfectly grounded explanation of a wrong rule is still wrong, so those rules need fixing first.

1 · How the fact checker works

Every DeepSeek recommendation now passes through aegis_verify.py before it reaches the dashboard. It does not ask another AI whether the text looks right. Each check compares the text mechanically against exactly what the model was given.

NumbersEvery number must appear in the crew readings, thresholds or evidence (rounding allowed; "about" allows 5%). Advice durations like "2 hours" are exempt.
CitationsEach sentence that cites [n] must be about the same thing as passage n (MiniLM similarity ≥ 0.30, the same model the knowledge base uses).
ActionsThe model must say which approved step each action rewrites, and the rewrite must stay close to that step (similarity ≥ 0.40).
MedicinesNo medicine, supplement or dose unless it already appears in the approved steps or evidence.

If a draft fails, the model gets one retry with the problems listed. If it fails again, the card shows the rule-based advice instead. Cards now carry a ✓ Fact-checked or Rules only badge, and hovering the badge shows the counts. Voice answers get the numbers check with the same one-retry rule.

SOLARIS's fixed thresholds (warning above 0.5, shelter above 1.0 mSv in 24 h) are below the normal background of every destination except low-end ISS. On a Mars transit the crew would be ordered to the storm shelter every day. A storm shelter is for solar particle events, which are sudden spikes many times above background. The rule should compare against the mission's expected background rate.

PULSE matches the definitions. One gap: PULSE only reacts to a slow heart rate below 40 bpm. The 50 bpm "low" threshold is defined in the code but never used.

Simulator radiation history

The simulated crew start at mission day 54 with 135–145 mSv already received. That implies about 2.6 mSv per day, which is 3 to 6 times the ISS rate and above deep-space cruise. A real ISS crew would be near 24–48 mSv by day 54. Because the agents project the yearly dose from this history, they tell mission control the crew are heading for about 1,240 mSv a year. The AI repeats this accurately, but the input itself is unrealistic.

4 · Does the checker catch hallucinations?

We took the 9 verified outputs and planted known errors in them. Each error is a realistic kind of mistake: a changed reading, an off-topic claim pinned to a real source, an action from another agent's protocol, or a medicine.

Planted errorCaughtMissed
Wrong or invented number20 / 20None
Off-topic claim with a real citation45 / 45None
Medicine or dose45 / 45None. The first run missed "antibiotics" (plural); that bug is fixed and re-scored.
Action not in the approved steps75 / 82"Do a hard cardio workout" passed as "reduce exertion". "Put on an oxygen mask" passed as "breathing exercises". "Extra hour of sleep" passed as "schedule a follow-up".
Clean outputs left untouched9 / 9 passNo false alarms on verified text

The action check is the weak point. Similarity scoring measures topic, not meaning, so it cannot tell "reduce exertion" from "do a hard workout". The calibration below, run on 59 real AI actions and 424 off-menu pairings, shows there is no threshold that both accepts friendly rewording and rejects opposite instructions:

The fix is a small entailment model, which checks whether one sentence means the same as another, in place of plain similarity. It needs a one-time download of about 70–180 MB, so we have not added it yet. The numbers on this page will change if the threshold is re-tuned on other data.

6 · What this does not prove

  • Small samples. 4 Inspiration4 crew, 10 recommendation scenarios and 12 questions. Each result shows a direction, not a measured accuracy rate.
  • The Inspiration4 mission lasted 3 days in low Earth orbit, and its blood was drawn on the ground. Long missions may behave differently.
  • PREDICT is not validated on real data. Its trend forecasts were tested on simulated vital-sign streams only. No continuous astronaut vital-sign dataset was used.
  • The citation check proves relevance, not truth. A sentence can be on the same topic as its source and still misstate it. 68 of 69 cited NASA reports now carry their real abstracts, which helps.
  • Units are not checked. One voice answer called mSv "milligray-ish units" in an earlier run. The number was right; the unit wording was not.

7 · Proposed fixes

None of these are applied yet. Each one changes how the demo behaves.

  1. GENE: add the personal-baseline rule (≥ 1.5× own baseline and +1.5 pg/mL) alongside the absolute threshold.
  2. SOLARIS: judge the daily dose against the mission's expected background (ISS ≈ 0.5, cruise ≈ 1.8, Mars surface ≈ 0.64 mSv/day). Warn at about 2× background and shelter only on a spike that looks like a solar particle event. Update the projection to NASA's 600 mSv career limit.
  3. Simulator: start the crew at a realistic dose for day 54 (about 30–100 mSv depending on destination).
  4. Action check: replace similarity with a small entailment model (needs a one-time download).
  5. PULSE: use the defined 50 bpm threshold as a "watch" level for slow heart rate.
